# Mallitalytics Growth Strategy on X — A Full Playbook for a Baseball Analytics Brand

This is a complete, actionable growth plan for **@Mallitalytics** built on what is verifiably working on X for baseball analytics, sports data, and visualization accounts in 2024–2026. Everything is grounded in (a) X's own publicly known engagement weights, (b) the public posting patterns of high-performing baseball accounts (PitchingNinja, Codify, FanGraphs, Baseball Savant, Sarah Langs/SlangsOnSports, Foolish Baseball, Just Baseball, MLBRandomStats, Pitching Ninja, Jomboy/CespedesBBQ, Eno Sarris, Dingertracker, etc.), and (c) sports-content posting-time and format research from 2024–2026.

A note on epistemic honesty: throughout this report, when I cite "what works," I'm describing patterns observable from the public output of those accounts and from X's open-sourced ranking signals — not from internal analytics those creators don't share. Treat all benchmarks as starting hypotheses to validate with your own analytics.

---

## TL;DR

- **Win the algorithm by chasing replies, bookmarks, and quote tweets — not likes.** X's open-sourced ranking gives replies that get author-replies a ~75x weight vs. a like, retweets ~20x, replies ~13.5x, and bookmarks ~10x. Mallitalytics should design every post around one of those four behaviors, not "what looks good."
- **Be the warm sabermetric voice in a feed of cold dashboards and loud takes.** The lane between Baseball Savant (cold data utility), PitchingNinja (visual entertainment), Codify (pitcher-focused, B2B-leaning), and FoolishBaseball/Just Baseball (storytelling/breakdown) is wide open. Mallitalytics' edge is **"data with a one-sentence narrative"** — every card answers *what changed and why it matters.*
- **Anchor the account on five recurring daily/weekly products** (Probables of the Day, HR Tracker, Player Card of the Day, Pitcher to Watch, Leaderboard Watch) and layer in event-driven content (live reactions, Statcast Signal, Why This Is Happening). Habit-forming series + explicit bookmark/discussion prompts are how analytics accounts actually compound followers in baseball.

## 1. What Currently Works on X for Baseball Analytics & Sports-Data Accounts

### 1a. Post types ranked by what they actually drive

Based on patterns from PitchingNinja (560K+ followers), Codify (143K+), FoolishBB (208K+), SlangsOnSports (47K+ posts), JustBB_Media, MLBRandomStats, and dozens of fantasy/analytics handles:

| Post type | Best for | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Single graphic / card** (1 image, 1 idea, 1 number) | Reposts, bookmarks, follows | Travels in QTs; readable in 1 second; shareable as proof |
| **Side-by-side comparisons** (Player A vs. Player B / This year vs. last year) | Replies, debate | Built-in argument; viewers feel compelled to side |
| **"What changed?" breakdown** (4–8 tweet thread, 1 graphic per beat) | Bookmarks, profile visits | Teaches → trust → follow |
| **GIF/Statcast clip + 1 stat overlay** (PitchingNinja format) | Reposts, virality | Visual + numeric; works on every kind of timeline |
| **Stat with hidden context** ("X is on pace for Y — and his xwOBA is 5th-best in MLB") | Replies, QTs | Surprise + verifiable hook |
| **Leaderboard screenshots with 1 sentence of editorial** | Bookmarks, debate | Bookmarkable reference + argument starter |
| **Live game reaction with Statcast number** | Replies, follows during peak hours | Rides search/trending volume |
| **Polls (binary, with stat backing)** | Replies, profile visits | Low-friction; algo loves engagement velocity |
| **Hot take supported by 3 numbers** | Reposts, QTs, follows | Personality + data = unique brand fingerprint |
| **Daily series posts** (Probables, HR Tracker) | Habit, returning visits | Trains the algorithm + the audience |

What does NOT lead the way for an analytics brand: pure text-only takes, link-in-tweet posts (X penalizes external links 30–50% in reach), generic "good night Twitter" posts, hashtag spam, and threads longer than ~10 tweets.

### 1b. Tone that performs best

Across the high-performing analytics accounts, three voices consistently win:

1. **Educational-but-confident** ("Here's the number, here's what it means, here's what to watch.") — Sarah Langs, FanGraphs, Just Baseball.
2. **Scout/data hybrid** (number + visual + a one-sentence "scouting" framing.) — PitchingNinja, Codify.
3. **Sharp + sourced** (a take, but every take backed by 2–3 data points.) — Foolish Baseball, Eno Sarris, MLBRandomStats.

What underperforms: Bloomberg-cold (no narrative), ESPN-loud (no data), and "insider" posturing without substance. Mallitalytics' stated voice — *warm, analytical, no empty opinions* — is exactly the gap.

## 2. What Does NOT Work for Analytics Accounts

These are the failure patterns visible across hundreds of low-performing analytics-style posts and consistent with X's algorithm signals:

- **Overcomplicated charts.** A 1200×675 card with 8 metrics, 4 colors, and 3 axes gets bookmarked by 0.1% of viewers and scrolled past by everyone else. Rule: **one chart, one takeaway, one number the eye lands on first.**
- **Acronym soup.** "Pitcher X has elite SIERA, xFIP, CSW%, and SwStr%" alienates ~70% of MLB fans. Translate: "His swing-and-miss is in the top 5%, and the stat that predicts ERA best agrees."
- **Weak hooks.** "Here are some interesting stats from last night." vs. "Aaron Judge has done something only Babe Ruth has done since 1927." The second wins by an order of magnitude.
- **Generic stat dumps with no takeaway.** "Player went 2-for-4 with a HR and 2 RBI" is a box score. Posting it adds nothing to a feed already saturated with MLB.com automation.
- **No story arc.** A graphic without a "so what" sentence in the post copy is a dead post.
- **Posting links in the main tweet.** X penalizes outbound links heavily — put the link in a reply, not the post body.
- **"Cold" branding.** Pure black background + Helvetica + no editorial language reads as a scraper. Warm typography + named series + a recurring sign-off compound recognition.
- **Inconsistency.** Algorithm research shows TweepCred (account reputation) suffers when posting cadence is uneven. Three-week gaps cost more than three-week streaks earn.
- **No reply discipline.** Per the X open-source code, an author replying to replies is the single most powerful weighted signal (~+75 vs. baseline). Accounts that post and disappear leave the biggest engagement multiplier on the table.

## 4. Audience Analysis — What MLB Fans on X Actually Want

There are four overlapping audiences for a baseball analytics brand on X. Mallitalytics needs to feed all of them weekly without losing voice.

**A. Casual / team-first MLB fans**
- Want: their player did something good, their team has an edge tonight, the league context that makes it cooler.
- Trigger to follow: *"I saw a fact about my team I couldn't get anywhere else, in a graphic I want to share."*
- What gets bookmarks: HR Tracker updates featuring their player, "rare-air club" stats.

**B. Advanced/sabermetric fans**
- Want: xwOBA, barrel rate, run value, pitch-shape data, regression analysis, leaderboards beyond surface stats.
- Trigger to follow: *"They explained xwOBA-vs-wOBA divergence cleanly, and I trust the framing."*
- What gets bookmarks: leaderboards, methodology one-pagers, "Under the Hood" explainers.

**C. Fantasy / DFS / betting players**
- Want: matchup edges, probables, weather, lineup volatility, buy/sell timing, breakout signal vs. noise.
- Trigger to follow: *"Their Probables thread saved me from rostering a trap start."*
- What gets bookmarks: Probables of the Day, Buy/Sell with Data, Matchup Edge.

**D. Prospect / dynasty / scout-curious fans**
- Want: minor-league translations, swing/pitch development signs, who's about to be called up.
- Trigger to follow: *"They flagged the breakout three weeks before the call-up."*
- What gets bookmarks: Breakout Watch, prospect cards.

The four behaviors (follow, reply, repost, bookmark) get triggered by different things. Engineer for each:

| Behavior | Triggered by |
|---|---|
| **Follow** | A whole *thread* or *card* that delivered enough value the user thinks "I want this once a day." Profile visit + clean bio + pinned post = conversion. Aim for 10–15% profile-visit-to-follow rate. |
| **Reply** | A clear opinion, a polarizing comparison, an open question, a misread invited ("Tell me which one you take"). |
| **Repost** | A stat that makes the reposter look smart for sharing. Rare-air clubs, leaderboards, surprising splits. |
| **Bookmark** | Something the user expects to need later: leaderboards, matchup grids, methodology, prospect lists. **Explicitly write "Bookmark this for later"** in the post copy when appropriate. |

## 5. Content Audit — Existing Mallitalytics Products

### Player Cards
**Diagnosis:** Player cards are bookmarkable by default. The trap is that without a story, they read as a stat block — pretty but forgettable.
**Fix:**
- Add one **"What changed?"** line above the card: *"Riley has cut his chase rate 7 points and his barrel rate is up 4 — here's the card."*
- Always include a **percentile band** (top 5%, top 15%, bottom 25%) — fans process percentiles faster than raw numbers.
- Include a **"signal vs. noise"** mini-flag: green dot = sustainable, yellow = mixed, red = regression risk. This single design element converts a card from data into a take.
- Cadence: 1/day during the season, with a thematic hook (rookie Monday, slumping Tuesday, breakout Wednesday, contender Thursday, "Saved You a Click" Friday).

### Pitcher Cards
**Diagnosis:** Pitcher cards have the highest "data volume" of any Mallitalytics product. The risk: too many pitches, too many splits, not enough narrative.
**Fix:**
- Lead with **the one pitch driving the story** (the new sweeper, the velo dip, the changed usage). Make that pitch's row visually heavier.
- Show **whiff/chase/contact-quality vs. league average** as three small tick bars, not a table.
- Add **"Watch for"** line — what a fan should see in the next start. This converts the card into a viewing companion.
- Show **xERA vs. ERA** clearly — divergence is the easiest narrative.
- Cadence: Pitcher to Watch every game day; deeper Pitcher Card 2–3x/week.

### Probables of the Day
**Diagnosis:** This is your highest-frequency habit-forming product. Done right, it's the daily "open Mallitalytics first" reason people follow.
**Fix:**
- Post by **9:00–10:30 AM ET** every game day — early enough for fantasy lineups, late enough for confirmed starters.
- Single carousel-style graphic of the slate or 2 graphics (early/late games). Each matchup gets **one "edge" line**.
- The hook is always: **"Today's Hidden Edge: ___"** — pick one matchup that the slate is sleeping on.
- End with a **daily question** to drive replies: *"Which start are you locking in?"*
- Reply to first 20 replies within 60 minutes — the ~+75 author-reply weight is your daily engagement multiplier.

### HR Tracker
**Diagnosis:** Crowded space (Dingertracker, longballstats, OnlyHomers, MLB.com). Mallitalytics needs a differentiated angle.
**Fix:**
- **Don't post every HR.** Post **only the HRs that mean something** — milestones, Statcast extremes (top 1% EV, hit a ball that's a HR in only 1 park, etc.), context HRs ("first since…").
- **Weekly HR Tracker leaderboard** Sunday night: top EV, longest, "HR in fewest parks," pace projections.
- **"Trending Toward a Power Breakout"** — twice a week, flag a hitter whose xSLG is climbing past their SLG.
- Use a fixed visual identity: charcoal background, ballpark silhouette in the corner, burnt orange for the distance number.

### Intel Leaderboards
**Diagnosis:** Leaderboards are inherently bookmarkable but often forgettable because they answer the wrong question.
**Fix:**
- Each leaderboard must answer **a question the user is already asking** ("Who's making the best contact since June 1?" not "Top 10 in xwOBA").
- Add a **"Mainstream rank vs. Mallitalytics rank"** column — this builds the brand as a corrective lens.
- Always include **a one-sentence editorial under the leaderboard:** "What this reveals: Tatis is the league's #4 contact-quality hitter and most fans still think he's slumping."
- Cadence: 2 leaderboards per week (Tues + Sat) is the right rhythm.

## 6. Editorial Strategy — 12 Content Pillars Fully Developed

For each: purpose, format, frequency, hook style, example, KPI.

### 1. Player Card of the Day
- **Purpose:** A daily "ritual" post that establishes editorial muscle and trains the audience to check Mallitalytics.
- **Format:** 1200×675 card with photo, 6–8 metrics, percentile bars, "what changed" line.
- **Frequency:** Daily, posted 11:30 AM–12:30 PM ET.
- **Hook style:** *"What changed for [Player]?"* / *"This is what a real breakout looks like."*
- **Example post copy:** "Junior Caminero hasn't 'figured it out' — he's been one of the league's hardest hitters all year. The card." → image → 1 sentence.
- **KPIs:** Replies, bookmarks, profile visits.

### 2. Pitcher to Watch
- **Purpose:** Game-day pitching focus; fantasy/DFS/betting magnet.
- **Format:** 1200×675 with arsenal heat-bars, recent-form sparkline, opponent-team weakness line.
- **Frequency:** Daily, 10:00 AM ET, paired with Probables of the Day.
- **Hook style:** *"The pitch that's quietly driving [Pitcher]'s breakout."*
- **Example:** "Tarik Skubal's changeup is now an 81-grade pitch by run value. Here's why hitters can't lay off."
- **KPIs:** Bookmarks, reposts.

### 3. Probables of the Day
- **Purpose:** The flagship daily habit. Recurring, expected, missable.
- **Format:** Single carousel graphic — all probables in slate order with a tiny edge tag on each.
- **Frequency:** Daily, 9:30–10:30 AM ET.
- **Hook style:** *"Today's slate. Hidden edge: ___."*
- **Example:** "Today's probables. Hidden edge: Cease vs. a Padres lineup with a 31% K-rate vs. sliders. Bookmark for later 🔖"
- **KPIs:** Bookmarks (primary), replies, repeat profile visits.

### 4. HR Tracker
- **Purpose:** Shareable, season-long tentpole product; drives reposts during HR moments.
- **Format:** Live single graphic per qualifying HR ("only top X% EV" / milestone / context HR) + weekly Sunday leaderboard.
- **Frequency:** Selectively in-game, ~3–7 graphics/day during the season; full leaderboard Sunday night.
- **Hook style:** *"He didn't just hit it. He hit it harder than anyone has in [stadium / season / etc.]."*
- **Example:** "Oneil Cruz: 122.9 mph. The hardest-hit ball of 2026. The only player to top 122 this year, and he's done it twice."
- **KPIs:** Reposts, follows.

### 5. Leaderboard Watch
- **Purpose:** Bookmarkable, debate-starting reference posts.
- **Format:** Top-15 list as a card, with one editorial sentence below.
- **Frequency:** 2x/week (Tuesday + Saturday).
- **Hook style:** *"What [stat] reveals that the rankings are missing."*
- **Example:** "Hard-hit% leaders since June 1. Notice who's not in the top 5."
- **KPIs:** Bookmarks, replies.

### 6. Statcast Signal
- **Purpose:** The "tip-off" pillar — flagging early changes before mainstream catches them.
- **Format:** Single image with two charts (rolling 30-day metric vs. season). One paragraph of copy.
- **Frequency:** 3x/week.
- **Hook style:** *"A change you should know about."*
- **Example:** "Cody Bellinger's average exit velo over his last 50 PAs is up 3.4 mph. That's not noise."
- **KPIs:** Profile visits, follows.

### 7. Buy/Sell with Data
- **Purpose:** Fantasy/DFS magnet; debate generator.
- **Format:** Two columns ("Buy" / "Sell") with 3 names each, each with one stat justifying the call.
- **Frequency:** Weekly (Sunday night or Monday AM).
- **Hook style:** *"Three you're underrating. Three you're overrating."*
- **Example:** "Buy: Spencer Strider — his slider whiff rate is back to 2023 levels. Sell: Bryan Reynolds — his xwOBA hasn't risen, his BABIP has."
- **KPIs:** Replies, bookmarks.

### 8. Why This Is Happening
- **Purpose:** Mid-length explainer that shows analytical depth.
- **Format:** 5–8 tweet thread with 2–3 graphics.
- **Frequency:** 1–2x/week.
- **Hook style:** *"Everyone's asking why [trend]. The data has an answer."*
- **Example:** "Everyone's asking why MLB strikeout rates are dropping for the first time since 2008. It's not the bat or the ball. It's pitch shape. A short thread →"
- **KPIs:** Bookmarks, profile visits.

### 9. One Chart, One Takeaway
- **Purpose:** The brand's signature simplicity statement.
- **Format:** Strict — one chart, one number highlighted, one sentence.
- **Frequency:** 4–5x/week.
- **Hook style:** *"One chart. One takeaway."*
- **Example:** "One chart. One takeaway. Skenes' fastball location is more concentrated than any starter in MLB."
- **KPIs:** Reposts.

### 10. Under the Hood
- **Purpose:** Educational/methodology pillar; builds trust + authority.
- **Format:** Short thread (4–6 tweets) explaining a metric or method, with one example player.
- **Frequency:** Weekly (Wednesday).
- **Hook style:** *"What [metric] actually measures — in one minute."*
- **Example:** "What 'barrel rate' actually measures, and why it's a better breakout indicator than HR pace."
- **KPIs:** Bookmarks (highest of any pillar), follows.

### 11. Matchup Edge
- **Purpose:** Game-day micro-product paired with Probables.
- **Format:** Single graphic showing pitcher's worst pitch vs. opponent's best-vs.-that-pitch hitters.
- **Frequency:** 2–4x per game day.
- **Hook style:** *"The matchup hiding in plain sight."*
- **Example:** "Sonny Gray throws his sweeper 32%. The Phillies have the lowest sweeper xwOBA in MLB. The matchup edge is real."
- **KPIs:** Reposts, replies.

### 12. Breakout Watch
- **Purpose:** Prospect / dynasty / scout-curious anchor.
- **Format:** Card profiling a prospect or under-rostered hitter; AAA/MLB stats + one Statcast hook.
- **Frequency:** 2x/week.
- **Hook style:** *"Three weeks before the call-up: ___."*
- **Example:** "Breakout Watch: Bryce Eldridge. 25% barrel rate in his MLB cup of coffee, 95.6 mph average EV. The Giants will need him soon."
- **KPIs:** Bookmarks, follows.

## 8. Turning Stats Into Stories — Per Content Type

This is the single most important capability for Mallitalytics. Every product needs a one-sentence narrative engine.

| Product | Narrative engine | Question being answered |
|---|---|---|
| **Player Card** | *What changed?* | What's different about this player vs. their baseline, and is it sustainable? |
| **Pitcher Card** | *What pitch is driving the breakout/problem?* | Identify the single pitch with the biggest run-value or whiff-rate change, then frame around it. |
| **Probables of the Day** | *Which matchup has the hidden edge?* | What does the slate look like through the lens of one stat the market is sleeping on? |
| **HR Tracker** | *Who is trending toward a power breakout?* | Use rolling EV, barrel%, pull%, launch-angle drift to flag breakouts before HR pace catches up. |
| **Leaderboard Watch** | *What does this leaderboard reveal that fans are missing?* | The editorial sentence is everything: who's surprisingly high, who's surprisingly low, and why. |
| **Statcast Signal** | *Has the input changed before the output?* | Inputs (EV, barrel%, chase%) lead outputs (BA, HR, OPS). Show the lead. |
| **Matchup Edge** | *Where does the pitcher's worst pitch meet the lineup's best response?* | One specific pitch + one specific lineup split. |
| **Breakout Watch** | *Why is this prospect/role player the next obvious add?* | Lead with the one Statcast or minor-league number that matters most. |

A simple in-house rule: **No card ships without a one-sentence "what changed and why it matters" caption.** This is the brand's narrative discipline.

## 9. Engagement Tactics

### 9a. Replies
- **Author-replies-to-replies is the #1 weighted signal.** Reply to ~20 of the first replies on each major post within 60 minutes.
- **Reply with substance, not "Thanks!"** Add a follow-up stat, a clarification, a "good question — here's what I'd flag too."
- **Reply with a *new graphic* when warranted.** A reply with a fresh card on the same player can re-trigger the algorithm and travel separately.
- **Don't reply to trolls; reply to the curious.** Engagement velocity matters; arguing with bad-faith accounts depresses your TweepCred.

### 9b. Quote tweets
- Use QTs for **(a) extending your own viral content**, **(b) adding data to a news/insider tweet**, **(c) respectful contrarian framing of a popular take**.
- 70/30 rule: ~70% original posts, ~30% engagement (QTs + replies + threads jumping into conversations).
- **QT-with-data formula:** "[insider's news]. Here's the relevant context: [one stat + one chart]."
- Avoid clout-chasing huge accounts only — QT creators of all sizes; a thoughtful QT to a 10K account is more likely to earn a mutual follow.

### 9c. Entering conversations without sounding like spam
- Search recurring phrases like *"who has the better matchup tonight,"* *"is X really for real,"* *"who should I roster"* — these are bookmark-magnet contexts.
- Reply with **one chart and one sentence** — never with "DM me" or "follow for more." Respect the conversation; the follow comes from value.
- Tag accounts only when relevant; never tag major accounts to draft on their following.

### 9d. Turning viral posts into follows
- **Pinned tweet must be your single best evergreen card** (e.g., a methodology explainer or "How to read a Mallitalytics card").
- **Bio = clear value statement** + (optional) site URL + 1 social-proof line ("Daily MLB analytics — Probables, HR Tracker, Player Cards").
- After a post breaks out, post a **follow-up** within 12 hours with a complementary card; viral-post viewers go to the profile, see something fresh, and convert.
- Aim for the algorithm-suggested 10–15% **profile-visit-to-follow** ratio. If lower, your bio/pinned post needs work — not your content.

### 9e. Questions, polls, debate prompts
- **Polls work** when the data is the answer. ("Higher xwOBA past 30 days: A or B?") — viewers vote, then you reveal in a reply with the chart. Doubles replies.
- **Open-ended prompts** at the end of every Probables thread: "Which start are you locking in?"
- **"Tell me which one you'd take"** is one of the highest-reply-rate phrasings; build it into recurring slots (Buy/Sell day).
- Don't overuse polls — 1 per week is plenty.

### 9f. Encouraging bookmarks
- **Explicitly write "Bookmark this for later 🔖"** on Probables slates, Matchup Edge, and Leaderboards. People bookmark when invited.
- Bookmarks weight ~10x a like — they're the cheapest engagement to engineer.
- Make sure bookmarks have *future* utility: probables for tonight, matchup for this week, leaderboard for this month. If it's not useful tomorrow, don't ask for the bookmark.

### 9g. Extending the life of strong content
- **Same-day:** Reply to the original post 4–6 hours later with a complementary card ("Following up — here's a related chart for context.")
- **Next-day:** Pull a quote from the post into a new tweet ("Yesterday's [stat] for [Player] got a lot of replies. Here's the deeper version.")
- **Next-week:** Re-share the original card in a "this week in Mallitalytics" weekly recap.
- **Next-month:** Roll the card into a monthly "Best of" thread.

## 10. Timing & Cadence

### 10a. Weekly posting frequency
For an analytics brand chasing growth from a small base, the right volume is **3–6 posts/day during the season**, **1–3/day in the offseason**. This includes 1 daily anchor product (Probables) + 2–3 editorial posts + selective live-game reactions.

Sports content on X consistently performs best **between 12 PM and 10 PM ET**, peaking at **4 PM and 8 PM ET**. Sports brands also see ~16% higher weekend engagement than weekdays. Sunday at 7 PM ET is one of the strongest sports-content windows of the week.

### 10c. Off-day / Monday strategy
Monday slates are thin. Use them for:
- **Under the Hood** (Wednesday's pillar can move to Monday during light slate weeks).
- **Why This Is Happening** thread.
- **Leaderboard Watch.**
- Deeper card formats that don't fit a busy game day.

### 10d. Postseason
- Post **before**, **during**, and **after** every playoff game.
- Pre-game: matchup card, pitcher arsenal, "X-factor" hitter.
- In-game: live Statcast reactions on every elimination-stage moment.
- Post-game: 1 graphic per high-impact moment + 1 series-state recap card.
- Postseason is when high-engagement velocity is most rewarded — your post can travel 3–5x further than regular season.

### 10e. Offseason (Nov–Feb)
- **Cadence:** 1–3 posts/day. Monday + Thursday should always have a tentpole post.
- **Pillars:** Hot Stove reaction with data, "What this signing actually means by the numbers," award debates, year-in-review breakdowns, prospect lists, projections.

- This is also when *Under the Hood* and *Why This Is Happening* threads do best — slower feeds, more dwell time.

The 2023 WBC saw +564% more total engagement on official accounts vs. 2017, and Ohtani crossed 4M Instagram followers during the event. The WBC is a once-every-4-years X opportunity and Mallitalytics should treat it like a mini-postseason.

## 11. Branding & Differentiation

### How Mallitalytics should sound
- **Confident, not loud.** "He's been the league's best contact-quality hitter since June 1" beats "INSANE STATS!! 🚨🚨"
- **Generous, not academic.** Define metrics in-line ("xwOBA — what a hitter's contact quality predicts his stats should be") so casual fans aren't excluded.
- **Grounded, not contrarian for sport.** Take positions, but only ones the data supports. The brand promise is *"data-driven baseball without empty opinions."* Live it.
- **Curious, not cynical.** Even when calling out a slumping team, frame as "here's what's interesting" not "here's what's broken."

### Recurring phrases that should become brand vocabulary
- **"Today's hidden edge: ___"** (Probables)
- **"What changed:"** (Player Cards)
- **"One chart. One takeaway."** (signature post type)
- **"Bookmark this. You'll want it later."** (CTA)
- **"Signal vs. noise."** (regression analysis frame)
- **"What the box score won't tell you."** (post-game)
- **"The data has an answer."** (Why This Is Happening)
- **"Three weeks before the call-up:"** (Breakout Watch)
- **"Mallitalytics rank vs. mainstream rank."** (Leaderboards)
- **Sign-off** at end of threads: *"Data-driven baseball, no empty opinions. — Mallitalytics."*

### What to avoid so it doesn't become a generic stats account
- **No naked stat dumps.** Every card needs a sentence.
- **No five-acronym sentences.** Translate one of them every time.
- **No copying PitchingNinja, Codify, or Savant aesthetic.** Charcoal background + burnt orange + warm typography = your fingerprint. Hold the line.
- **No engagement bait** (counter-takes for the sake of it, "1 like = 1 vote" posts, follow-for-follow.)
- **No reposting other accounts' analysis without QT-attribution + your own value-add.**

### Blending advanced stats with accessible language
- Always pair an advanced metric with a translation in parentheses: "xwOBA (what his contact says he should be hitting)…"
- Use **percentiles** liberally — "top 5%" lands faster than ".423."
- When using a niche metric (CSW%, run value, swing decision %), include a one-line "Under the Hood" link in the reply.
- Visualize wherever possible — a percentile bar or sparkline does more work than three sentences of explanation.

## 12. 30-Day Execution Plan

### Week 1 — Establish the anchor and the look
- **Objective:** Lock in the daily Probables habit and visual identity. Build a baseline of 5–6 daily posts.
- **Experiments:** A/B test two Probables formats (single carousel slate vs. one-graphic-per-matchup). Track bookmarks on each.
- **Schedule:**
  - Daily: Probables (10:00 AM ET), Pitcher to Watch (11:30 AM), Player Card (12:30 PM), 1 evening reaction.
  - Wed/Sat: Leaderboard Watch.
  - Sunday: HR Tracker weekly leaderboard.
- **Metrics to track:** Profile-visit-to-follow rate, bookmarks per post, replies per post, reply rate from author, follower delta.
- **Hypothesis:** The single carousel Probables slate gets more bookmarks but fewer replies; one-per-matchup gets more replies. Pick the one that better serves growth.
- **Success criteria:**
  - Profile-visit-to-follow ≥ 8%
  - At least 2 posts/week with 50+ bookmarks
  - Reply rate from author ≥ 80% on top 20 replies
- **Double down on:** whichever Probables format wins; the day's best-performing post type — replicate it 1 day later.

### Week 2 — Launch the editorial pillars
- **Objective:** Test the 12 pillars against each other; identify the 4–5 highest-ROI ones.
- **Experiments:**
  - Mon: Leaderboard Watch (engineered for bookmarks).
  - Tue: Statcast Signal (engineered for profile visits).
  - Wed: Under the Hood thread (engineered for follows).
  - Thu: Buy/Sell (engineered for replies).
  - Fri: Why This Is Happening thread.
  - Sat: One Chart, One Takeaway.
  - Sun: Breakout Watch.
- **Metrics:** Same as Week 1 + per-pillar averages. Compare bookmarks/replies/follows across pillars.
- **Hypothesis:** Under the Hood and Buy/Sell will outperform on follows; Leaderboard Watch and Probables on bookmarks; Statcast Signal on profile visits.
- **Success criteria:**
  - Identify your top 3 pillars by follow conversion.
  - Week-over-week follower growth ≥ 3%.
  - At least one post crosses 200K impressions.
- **Double down on:** the top 3 pillars — increase to 2x/week each in Week 3.

### Week 3 — Scale wins, prune losers, run the engagement engine
- **Objective:** Double weekly volume on top pillars; cut bottom pillars; install systematic engagement protocols.
- **Experiments:**
  - Run the **author-reply-to-replies protocol** rigorously (20 replies in first 60 min on every post).
  - Test **two thread structures** for Under the Hood: 5-tweet "explainer" vs. 8-tweet "case study with a player." Compare bookmarks.
  - Test a **weekly poll** on Tuesday at 7 PM ET tied to the Statcast Signal.
  - Test a **QT week** — every QT must add a chart, not just commentary.
- **Schedule:** Increase to 6–7 posts/day. Probables anchor remains. Top 3 pillars each appear 2x.
- **Metrics:** Reply count and reply velocity (first-30-minute replies); QT growth from Mallitalytics; engagement-to-impression ratio per pillar.
- **Hypothesis:** Author-reply discipline will lift average post reach by ≥ 30%. The 8-tweet "case study" thread structure will outperform.
- **Success criteria:**
  - Average reach/post ≥ 30% above Week 1.
  - Profile-visit-to-follow ≥ 12%.
  - 1 post crosses 500K impressions.
- **Double down on:** whichever thread structure wins; whichever pillar has the highest engagement/impression ratio.

### Week 4 — Build for compounding: pinned-post optimization, cross-pillar packaging, and the first "tentpole"
- **Objective:** Stop being a stream of posts; start being a *body of work.*
- **Experiments:**
  - **Update the pinned tweet** to the highest-bookmarked Under the Hood thread of Week 2–3 (or the cleanest Buy/Sell).
  - **First weekly recap thread (Sunday):** "Top 5 charts from Mallitalytics this week." This recycles bookmarks-bait into a fresh post.
  - **Tentpole post:** Build one "season-defining" piece — e.g., *"The 10 hitters whose 2026 isn't real — and the 10 whose it is."* Card-driven thread, 8–10 graphics. This is a follow-magnet.
  - **Cross-pillar packaging:** Mention the Probables habit in your Under the Hood thread; mention Leaderboard Watch in your Buy/Sell.
- **Schedule:** Maintain Week 3 cadence + Sunday weekly recap.
- **Metrics:** Pinned-post profile-visit-to-follow conversion; tentpole post total impressions and follows; weekly-recap repost rate.
- **Hypothesis:** Pinned-post optimization alone will add 10–20% to the conversion rate. The tentpole post will generate more follows than any single Week 1–3 post.
- **Success criteria:**
  - Net follower gain in Week 4 ≥ 50% above Week 1.
  - Tentpole post crosses 1M impressions or generates ≥ 200 follows in 48 hours.
  - Pinned post drives ≥ 20% of week's follows.
- **Double down on:** whichever tentpole format works — schedule one every 3 weeks going forward (this is the rhythm Just Baseball, Foolish, and Codify use).

### What to do at the end of 30 days
- Build a **content map** of all 30 days' posts — top 10 by bookmarks, top 10 by replies, top 10 by follow conversion. Codify the patterns into a Mallitalytics playbook.
- Lock in the **anchor schedule** (Probables, Pitcher to Watch, Player Card daily; Leaderboard, Buy/Sell, Under the Hood, Statcast Signal, Why This Is Happening, Breakout Watch in fixed weekly slots).
- Set 60-day stretch goals: a **tentpole post once every 3 weeks**, a **methodology page** in the bio link to drive the curious, and **two scheduled live takeover days** per month (e.g., Opening Day, Trade Deadline).

## Caveats

- **Engagement weights from X's open-source code** (replies-to-replies +75, retweets ~+20, replies +13.5, profile clicks +12, link clicks +11, bookmarks +10, likes +0.5 to +1) are widely cited from the 2023 source release; the January 2026 xAI/Grok update is reported to have changed the model architecture but the directional weights are still the working consensus among growth practitioners. Treat the *direction* as reliable and the exact numbers as a useful approximation, not gospel.
- **Posting-time ranges** (sports peaks 12 PM–10 PM ET; 8 PM 3–4x more engagement than 8 AM; weekends ~16% higher) come from cross-industry data (Comscore, Sprout Social, Buffer, SocialPilot) — they are *baseline hypotheses* to validate against Mallitalytics' own analytics over 30–60 days.
- **Follower counts cited** for benchmark accounts (PitchingNinja ~560K, FanGraphs ~231K, FoolishBB ~208K, Codify ~143K, SlangsOnSports per public profile) are pulled from public profile pages and may have shifted by single-digit percentages by the time you read this.
- **Specific account behaviors** (e.g., Codify's pinned post, Just Baseball's "rare-air" framing, SlangsOnSports' "weekly notes") are observable from public output but are not strategy documents — they're inferences. Don't treat them as the only ways those accounts win.
- **Premium/non-premium reach gap is real but variable.** Multiple guides report Premium accounts get 4x in-network and 2x out-of-network boosts and that non-Premium accounts posting links see near-zero reach. Mallitalytics should run X Premium/Verified Organizations from day one given these dynamics, but the exact multipliers vary across sources.
- **WBC engagement growth (+564% in 2023 vs. 2017)** is from MLB.com's own self-reporting; the directional trend is clear, but the figure is a marketing claim, not an independently audited number.
- **What will *not* be predictable** is which specific posts go viral. The framework above is engineered to maximize the *probability* of viral hits and steady follower compounding — not to guarantee any individual post's outcome. The 30-day plan is built around iteration: track, prune, double down.
- Finally: every "best practice" here works in service of a brand voice. The single biggest risk is letting tactics flatten the *warm, analytical, no-empty-opinions* identity. When in doubt, choose the post that sounds most like Mallitalytics, not the one that scores highest in a generic engagement playbook. The voice is the moat.
