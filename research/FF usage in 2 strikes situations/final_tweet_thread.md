# X Thread Draft: Two-Strike Four-Seam Trust

1. I had a pitching-card question stuck in my head:

Do pitchers who still trust the four-seamer with two strikes tend to be better?

The intuition: when the hitter is protecting, a bad fastball gets exposed. A good one still plays.

2. So I tested it.

For each pitcher, I looked at:

- 2-strike FF%
- K%
- BB%
- K-BB%
- run value / 100
- xwOBA on BBE
- FF velo
- FF IVB / HB
- FF whiff
- FF CSW

3. Important methodology note:

Because this is a usage rate, denominator matters.

30% FF on 20 two-strike pitches is not as reliable as 30% FF on 300.

So I looked at both unweighted correlations and correlations weighted by each pitcher's two-strike pitch count.

4. The conclusion:

Two-strike FF% is not a magic success metric.

But it is a real trust signal.

Pitchers who use more four-seamers with two strikes tend to have better four-seam traits.

5. In the 2025 local sample:

2K FF% vs K-BB%:

- unweighted r = +0.192
- weighted r = +0.199

That is not huge, but it is positive.

Image: `x_images/03_ff_usage_vs_kbb_scatter.png`

6. The stronger signal was fastball quality.

2K FF% vs:

- FF velo: weighted r = +0.294
- FF IVB: weighted r = +0.335
- FF CSW%: weighted r = +0.316

That is the story.

Image: `x_images/02_weighted_correlations.png`

7. The best version of the finding:

Pitchers do not become good because they throw more four-seamers with two strikes.

They throw more four-seamers with two strikes because their four-seamer is good enough to survive there.

8. The physics layer matters.

IVB showed a stronger relationship than HB in this sample.

That makes sense: the two-strike four-seamer is often about staying above barrels, missing bats, and winning in the top/edge lanes.

9. The broader "fastball family" bucket did not carry the same signal.

FF/SI/FC together was weaker.

That matters because a four-seamer, sinker, and cutter are not doing the same job in two-strike counts.

10. My takeaway for pitching cards:

2-strike FF% should not be read as "throw more heaters."

It should be read as:

"Does this pitcher have a four-seamer he trusts when the at-bat reaches its decision point?"

11. That is a useful scouting/content signal.

High 2K FF% + good velo/IVB/CSW = trust backed by traits.

High 2K FF% without quality = possible danger.

Low 2K FF% with elite results = different shape of pitcher, likely secondary-driven.

Image: `x_images/04_trust_backed_by_traits_table.png` sorted by 2K FF%

12. Final line:

Two-strike four-seam usage is not the answer by itself.

It is a clue.

And the clue gets much louder when it comes with ride, velocity, and bat-missing performance.
