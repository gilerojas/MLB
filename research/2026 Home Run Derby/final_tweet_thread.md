# 2026 Home Run Derby Power Profiles

Data cutoff: games through July 12, 2026.

## 1/9

No clock. Just 20 swings in Round 1.

Which power profile is built for tonight's Home Run Derby?

I compared all eight hitters on one common scale, then looked deeper into how each one creates damage.

Image: `hr_derby_2026_power_radar.png`

## 2/9

Kyle Schwarber is the volume favorite.

Field-high 32 HR, a 19.4% barrel rate and a 404-ft average homer. Add 77.1 mph average bat speed and this is not just a loft profile. He brings force, lift and the most proven home-run production in the field.

Image: `02_kyle_schwarber.png`

## 3/9

Ben Rice is the efficiency outlier.

He has 29 HR despite a 72.6 mph average bat speed and 389-ft average homer, both near the bottom of this field. His path is different: create the right launch window repeatedly and let contact quality do the work.

Image: `03_ben_rice.png`

## 4/9

Junior Caminero owns the loudest raw swing.

79.9 mph average bat speed. An 88.2% fast-swing rate. A 116.9 mph max EV and a 463-ft longest homer.

In a fixed-swing format, nobody in this field can create more with one swing.

Image: `04_junior_caminero.png`

## 5/9

Jordan Walker has the field's hardest average contact.

His 94.2 mph average EV comes with 79.2 mph average bat speed and a 116.6 mph max EV. The 22-HR total trails the favorites, but the underlying power ingredients absolutely do not.

Image: `05_jordan_walker.png`

## 6/9

Munetaka Murakami has the strongest contact-quality blend.

59.3% hard-hit rate. 20.3% barrel rate. 94.1 mph average EV. His average homer has traveled 409 feet.

He does not need to manufacture Derby power. It is already present in his normal contact.

Image: `06_munetaka_murakami.png`

## 7/9

Willson Contreras is the deceptive distance profile.

His overall average EV is only 90.6 mph, but his homers average 407 feet with a 31.7-degree launch angle.

When he gets the ball into his home-run window, it carries much better than the overall EV suggests.

Image: `07_willson_contreras.png`

## 8/9

Bryce Harper brings the known Derby variable: the stage.

He has already reached 457 feet this season, owns 20 HR and will hit in front of Philadelphia.

The full-season power indicators are not the field's best. The setting, experience and one-swing ceiling still matter.

Image: `08_bryce_harper.png`

## 9/9

Jac Caglianone may be the best pure distance bet.

His average homer leads the field at 414 feet, and those homers leave the bat at a field-best 108.0 mph. Add a 116.1 mph max EV and 56.0% hard-hit rate.

My raw-power picks: Caminero and Caglianone. Who wins tonight?

Image: `09_jac_caglianone.png`

## Methodology

- Season and contact data: MLB Stats API and the production Statcast warehouse through July 12.
- Average bat speed follows Statcast's competitive-swing definition: the fastest 90% of a player's swings, plus swings of at least 60 mph that produced at least 90 mph exit velocity.
- Fast-swing rate is the share of competitive swings at 75 mph or faster.
- Hard-hit rate uses batted balls with recorded exit velocity as its denominator.
